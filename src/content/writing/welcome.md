An engineering project is more than its final result.

The useful part is often everything that happens before the result: the decisions, failed approaches, experiments, debugging sessions, architectural changes, and lessons that would otherwise disappear once the code works.

**Alok Engineering Lab exists to preserve that process.**

This is my personal engineering laboratory and long-term technical archive.

## What I am building

I am interested in building systems rather than only using them.

That means working across areas such as:

* software engineering
* automation
* artificial intelligence
* Linux and system experimentation
* developer tooling
* architecture and infrastructure
* technical research
* small prototypes and experiments

Some projects will become useful tools. Others may remain experiments. Some ideas will fail completely.

That is part of the Lab.

## Why document the work?

Building something once is useful.

Being able to understand **why it was built that way** months or years later is even more useful.

The Lab is therefore intended to capture more than polished outcomes. I want to record:

* what I tried
* what worked
* what failed
* why decisions changed
* what I learned
* what I would do differently
* how ideas evolve over time

The goal is not to make every entry look finished.

The goal is to make the engineering process understandable.

## How the Lab is organized

The Lab has several different layers.

**Projects** contain larger systems and substantial engineering work.

**Writing** contains more polished technical explanations and discoveries.

**Notes** contain smaller pieces of engineering knowledge, principles, observations, and ideas that are worth keeping.

**Experiments** are where prototypes, tests, benchmarks, and technical explorations can live without needing to become finished projects.

**Timeline** records the journey itself: important milestones, changes, and moments in the evolution of the Lab.

The **Digital Garden** connects the smaller pieces of this knowledge together.

## Building in public, learning privately

This is not intended to be a perfect portfolio.

It is a working laboratory.

Some entries will be rough. Some will eventually become much better. Old ideas may be corrected when new evidence appears.

That is intentional.

Engineering knowledge changes when we build, test, observe, and learn.

I want this archive to reflect that reality rather than hide it.

## The journey starts here

This first entry is intentionally simple: it marks the beginning of the archive.

There is a lot more to build.

There will be projects, experiments, technical notes, failures, improvements, and lessons along the way.

This Lab is where I will keep them.
